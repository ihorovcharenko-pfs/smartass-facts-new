Mark Twain's Real Name (and the Riverboat Call Behind It)

By SmartAss Facts Team · Last updated 2026-07-28 · More famous people →

⚡ Quick Answer

Mark Twain's real name was Samuel Langhorne Clemens. "Mark twain!" was a Mississippi riverboat leadsman's call meaning the water was two fathoms deep — twelve feet, safe for a steamboat. Clemens, a licensed river pilot before he was a writer, first signed the name in February 1863 while reporting for a Nevada newspaper.

From Riverboat Call to Pen Name

Before literature, Samuel Clemens had what he called the proudest job of his life: licensed steamboat pilot on the Mississippi (from 1859). Depth soundings were shouted up from the leadsman in fathoms — and "by the mark, twain!" meant two fathoms, the comfortable minimum for a steamboat. When Clemens needed a byline at Virginia City's Territorial Enterprise in February 1863, he reached back to the river.

It was not his first attempt at a pen name — he had earlier written as "Josh" and, magnificently, as "Thomas Jefferson Snodgrass." The river name is the one that stuck.

Born and Died with Halley's Comet

Clemens was born on November 30, 1835, two weeks after Halley's Comet reached perihelion. In 1909 he told his biographer: "I came in with Halley's Comet in 1835. It is coming again next year, and I expect to go out with it." He died on April 21, 1910 — one day after the comet's return perihelion. It remains one of the strangest true timing coincidences in literary history.

Bankruptcy and the Comeback Tour

Twain earned fortunes from his books and lost one investing roughly $300,000 in the Paige Compositor, a mechanical typesetting machine outrun by the Linotype. When his publishing house failed in 1894, he was not legally required to repay his creditors in full — but he insisted on it, embarking on a round-the-world lecture tour in his sixties. The debts were cleared by 1898, and the comeback made him more famous than ever.

10 Quick Mark Twain Facts

  • Real name: Samuel Langhorne Clemens — "Mark Twain" is a riverboat depth call.
  • Born (1835) and died (1910) within days of Halley's Comet's passes — and predicted it.
  • Held a steamboat pilot's license, the job he called his life's proudest.
  • Earlier pen names included "Josh" and "Thomas Jefferson Snodgrass."
  • Held three U.S. patents — including a self-pasting scrapbook that actually made money.
  • Invented a history trivia board game, "Mark Twain's Memory-Builder."
  • Went bankrupt backing a typesetting machine, then repaid every creditor in full by 1898.
  • Was friends with Nikola Tesla and was photographed experimenting in Tesla's lab.
  • Received an honorary doctorate from Oxford in 1907 — and wore the gown at every opportunity.
  • Claimed to be the first author to deliver a typewritten book manuscript to a publisher.

Frequently Asked Questions

What was Mark Twain's real name?

Samuel Langhorne Clemens, born November 30, 1835, in Florida, Missouri, and raised in Hannibal — the river town that became the model for St. Petersburg in Tom Sawyer and Huckleberry Finn.

Why did he choose the name 'Mark Twain'?

It is Mississippi riverboat jargon. The leadsman measuring depth would call "by the mark, twain!" when the line showed two fathoms — twelve feet, safe water for a steamboat. Clemens had been a licensed river pilot, and he adopted the call as his byline in February 1863.

Did Mark Twain really predict his own death?

Essentially, yes. In 1909 he said he came in with Halley's Comet and expected to go out with it. He was born two weeks after the comet's 1835 perihelion and died one day after its 1910 perihelion — a genuine, well-documented coincidence.

Was Mark Twain rich?

Repeatedly rich and once spectacularly broke. His books and lectures earned fortunes, but bad investments — above all the Paige typesetting machine — bankrupted his publishing business in 1894. A world lecture tour repaid every creditor in full by 1898.
