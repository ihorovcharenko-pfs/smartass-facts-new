Your MBTI type tells you what career is right for you. Wrong.

By SmartAss Facts Team · Last updated 2026-10-13 · More myths debunked →

⚡ Quick Answer

🔑 Key Takeaway

"Your MBTI type tells you what career is right for you" is a myth. The supporting research is cited below. Read on for the full evidence.

✗ The Myth

"Your MBTI type tells you what career is right for you"

✓ The Reality

False. The British Psychological Society rates MBTI as low in both reliability and validity for its stated purpose.

Millions of people have taken the Myers-Briggs Type Indicator. Employers use it in hiring. Career coaches build entire frameworks around INFJ vs. ESTP. If you've ever introduced yourself by your four letters, you're in very large and very misinformed company.

The Surprising Truth

The British Psychological Society rates MBTI as low in both reliability and validity for its stated purpose. Studies find approximately 50% of test-takers receive a different four-letter result when retested just five weeks later — making it less stable than many mood questionnaires. More critically, MBTI has near-zero predictive validity for job performance, which is supposedly what it helps optimize. A 2014 analysis in Personality and Individual Differences found MBTI fails standard psychometric criteria. This is why research psychologists use the Big Five personality model instead — it has decades of validated peer-reviewed predictive data across careers, health outcomes, and relationships.

What This Means for You

If you've been making career decisions based on your MBTI type, a validated alternative like the Big Five or HEXACO will give you meaningfully more accurate and stable results. Your four letters may tell you something — just not reliably the same thing twice.
